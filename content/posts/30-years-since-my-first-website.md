It hit me this week that 2026 marks 30 years since I put my first website online.

It's been a pretty hectic year for me, for all sorts of reasons, both personal and professional. I let an anniversary slip by that, looking back now, feels like an important milestone in my life.

By 1996 I already knew how to code. I'd written a few things in BASIC and Turbo Pascal for a BBS I set up in 1994. A BBS was a bulletin board system you could dial into with a modem. Back then I realized that if I wanted my BBS to work the way I'd imagined, I'd have to learn to write my own programs.

I got my first taste of the Web a little later, in 1995, when the internet started becoming available to the general public in Brazil.

As soon as I had the chance, I signed up with Nutecnet, a nationwide internet service provider. It was my first ISP.

In 1996 I switched to PeopleNet, a local provider in Brasília, on the recommendation of my friend Gustavo Edin, aka Badduqe. He went to the same college as Mario, the provider's sysadmin and webmaster, better known as Papasmurf.

I remember we played a lot of Duke Nukem. We played so much Quake we even had a clan called the DF EarthQuakers. DF stands for Distrito Federal, where Brasília is. And the Quake in EarthQuakers? That’s the game. Get it? :)

Mario had built the PeopleNet website and had a personal page of his own. One day I asked how he'd done it, and he gave me some HTML tutorials put together by RNP, Brazil's National Research Network. They were the same ones he'd learned from.

That's how I started learning HTML and put my first site online in 1996, using some web space that came with my PeopleNet account.

**peoplenet.com.br/~medrado**

## From PeopleNet to GeoCities

In 1997, for some reason I can't remember, I switched providers again. My site was still hosted by my old ISP, so I had to find somewhere else to put it.

I did what a lot of people did back then. I moved to GeoCities. ;)

The funny thing is, I still remember the address.

**geocities.com/Broadway/5804**

GeoCities offered free hosting and had an interesting idea. It organized sites into virtual neighborhoods, each devoted to a different set of topics.

Broadway, for example, was for pages about theater, musicals, and that sort of thing. There were plenty of other neighborhoods and categories to choose from.

Why did I pick Broadway? I have absolutely no idea. :)

Like any self-respecting '90s personal website, mine had the works. Animated GIFs, MIDI music that played automatically, and other wonders that would probably give a UX specialist a small nervous breakdown today.

My About page, for example, played the theme from *Terminator 2*.

And there was Java, too.

I started learning the language in 1996 after buying a book called *Aprenda Java em 60 minutos* (*Learn Java in 60 Minutes*). It taught me how to write applets, little Java programs that ran inside a web page, and of course I put a few on the site. If the browser could run a Java applet, why let the opportunity go to waste? :D

The site was basically a collection of everything I liked or found interesting at the time.

There was Harley-Davidson, Santos FC (the Brazilian soccer club), Shaquille O'Neal, Quake, MIDI music, HTML, and *Highlander*, a movie I was a big fan of. Still am, actually.

I also put up the same HTML tutorials Papasmurf had given me. In a way, I was just passing along what someone had shared with me.

## A time capsule (and a bit of nonsense)

While going through what I'd managed to recover from that site, I found the text of my old About page.

It's a real time capsule. I was 17 and, apparently, had no concern whatsoever for my future reputation.

I started by explaining that my nickname on IRC (Internet Relay Chat) was SpacE, short for SpacE GhosT, because the full name was too long. Funny enough, that old nickname is where spacexnu, the handle I still use today, came from years later.

Then came a short introduction in which I made a point of clarifying:

> *"I'm not Totally crazy... but I must be a little messed up in the head!!"*

Thirty years later, I'd rather not comment.

Then I went on about my hobbies, the kung fu tournaments I'd competed in, and my seven dogs, each duly introduced by name and nickname: Laika (Laikinha), Nick (Nick-Loo), BoogieLoogie (Bugalu), Dolly (Dolly-Doo), Cyrus (Cyrãos), Queen (Queen-Loo), and Jake (Jakão).

Seven dogs apparently weren't enough. They all needed stage names, too.

The best part came at the end, when I decided to share a few of my big dreams for the future.

Among them were becoming a sultan, having a harem of more than 500 women, owning a brewery, and finally, having a **Boogaloo Xeroso**.

What's a Boogaloo Xeroso?

I have absolutely no idea.

I mean, I know where it came from. It was a completely meaningless phrase I made up and kept repeating just to get on people's nerves. I never explained what it meant, and to this day nobody knows.

Thirty years later, I think it's best to keep it that way.

Good times. :)

## The sparrows of Brasília

The site also had a slightly odd section listing every speed camera in Brasília.

Back then we called them *pardais*, Portuguese for “sparrows.” Many of the cameras were tucked out of sight. I got my hands on a list of their locations and decided to put it on the site.

Apparently, my dislike of the government's knack for finding new ways to take money out of people's pockets goes back a long way.

The last time I updated that site was **August 4, 1997**.

It stayed up on GeoCities for years, until Yahoo, which had bought the service, shut it down on October 26, 2009.

A huge number of personal pages simply vanished from the internet around that time.

By then I'd already made other sites, tried other hosting services, and registered other domains. One of them was **neshertech.net**.

Later came **adlermedrado.com.br**, which I registered on May 5, 2008. It's still my personal address today. More recently, I also registered **spacexnu.com**.

## Thirty years later

A lot has changed since then.

I've watched technologies come along, take over the market, and disappear. I've watched the internet go from a place cobbled together by curious people and enthusiasts to something that's part of just about every aspect of our lives.

And I'm still here, writing code, learning new things, and keeping my own little corner of the Web.

This is simply a way to mark a date that nearly slipped by. A look back at my career or the whole history of the internet would be a different story.

**Thirty years.**

That's a long time, although when I think back to those days, it doesn't feel quite that long.

Unfortunately, I don't have complete, reliable backups of my old BBS or of all the websites I ran over the years. A lot got lost along the way. Sometimes I was careless, sometimes I switched computers, and sometimes it just never occurred to me that I'd miss those files one day.

I did manage to save a few things from the GeoCities site, at least.

When I look at it now, I see a bunch of rudimentary HTML, images, animated GIFs, applets, and a collection of interests that probably didn't make much sense together.

But it was mine. I made it the way I wanted it.

And maybe that's what I like remembering most.

Thanks for making it this far and spending a few minutes getting to know a little piece of my story.

**Thirty years later, I'm still here.**
